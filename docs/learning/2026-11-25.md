## Concepts to understand

A control variate subtracts a correlated quantity with known integral, reducing
integration error without changing the quantity being estimated. An exactly zero
integral on paper is insufficient when floating-point evaluation cannot resolve
the feature: normalizing a tiny rounded difference can magnify its error.

Numerical precision, numerical coverage, reproducibility and player attribution
are different claims. An estimator may have small point error but an uncertainty
allowance too large to certify precision. Replay can reproduce the same biased
answer. A small repeated-trial screen can miss undercoverage. Even full numerical
coverage would establish neither player uncertainty calibration nor causal value.

The original design's description of Bonferroni as "stricter" is corrected in the
evaluation notes: spreading a false-flag budget across more tests lowers each
alpha and therefore power. No frozen test or result is changed. A screen pass
means no severe flag under that rule, not demonstrated nominal coverage.

Next freeze a bounded work/precision/convergence study of the unchanged repaired
coarse integrator. Fix alternatives, caps, fresh randomizations and representative
known answers before results. Measure cost and precision rather than assume a
sample increase will pass. Preserve all consumed cases as regressions; no automatic
top-ups, diagnostic estimator selection or gate changes. Full numerical coverage,
archived comparison, the unfinished 192-fit player study and observational
admission remain on the scoring path.
