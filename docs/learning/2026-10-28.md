## Statistical concepts

**Coverage depends on assumptions.** A nominal 95% interval should contain its
target about 95% of the time under its stated repeated-data experiment. Drawing
truth from the model's own prior checks average behavior in that assumed
population; it does not guarantee coverage for a particular strong player.
The primary intervals cover 94.85%/94.94% in the 2025/2026 matched-prior controls.
With the frozen illustrative stronger effects, coverage drops to 0.96–1.94% and
0.88–1.13%. Those figures describe simulations, not actual NFL player errors.
All four roles fail the prespecified severe-undercoverage flag in these scenarios.

**Precision around the wrong center is insufficient.** Ridge pulls weakly
identified effects toward the prior mean. In the fixed-signal cases, mean absolute
conditional bias is 8.11/8.15 cumulative EP. Game-cluster CR0 estimates a sampling
variance around a biased estimator; it does not account for that bias or turn an
unobserved benchmark parameter into measured information. It also assumes
independent games, which persistent team shocks violate. At the primary penalty,
its overall fixed-signal coverage remains below 0.3%. Even the true simulated noise
covariance cannot rescue intervals centered on the biased estimate.

The separate oracle expectation control removes the *known synthetic* bias for
diagnosis. Its 94.76–95.14% range across all 24 cells supports the numerical
implementation. Real football has no known truth to supply that correction.
Lower penalty 10 has conservative iid coverage but only about 85.3% with persistent
team noise. These results are not permission to retune on a favorable scenario.

**Dependence also matters when measuring a simulation.** Every player in a draw
shares outcomes, teammates and benchmark estimates. Monte Carlo SE therefore uses
the variation of each complete replicate's coverage fraction, not a binomial
calculation pretending every player-by-draw cell is independent.

**Identifiability concerns the specific contrast.** Shifting a never-observed
actor coefficient changes no expected play outcomes. Six of 41 historical and
nine of 42 current prior-only shifts change the selected benchmark contrasts;
the largest single +0.25-EP shift changes a cumulative contrast by 0.180/0.164 EP.
The two exactly identical observed actor-column pairs in 2026 affect none of the
selected positional contrasts. The report preserves this negative result instead
of claiming those pairs prove ambiguity in every displayed contrast. This bounded
check does not exhaust near-collinearity or team/player confounding.

and evaluates batches of synthetic responses. There are 6,144 response fits, not
6,144 separate NFL samples. Immutable JSON reports bind the plan, producer,
source chain, environment, game/evidence cutoffs and diagnostic object hashes.
Replay rebuilds upstream cohorts/models and reproduces every simulation metric.
No pickles, database, new CI framework or publication pointer is introduced.

algebra and the existing joint fitter. Other tests cover singular systems,
prior-only versus sampling uncertainty, correlated Monte Carlo cells, fixed
seeds, synthetic-truth independence from actual EPA, source corruption, rehashed
metric tampering, false admission flags, version mismatch and prefix selection.
