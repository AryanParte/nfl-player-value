## Concepts and decisions

Computational allocation is not player-value allocation. If one proposal component
receives more samples, its mixture mass still determines its contribution to the
integral. Pooling by sample counts would change the quantity being estimated.

A ratio's error depends on numerator/denominator covariance. The pilot measures
`N - c*Z`, where `c` is estimated independently of final evaluation draws. It
cannot use known answers or pick whichever final estimate looks best. The
inverse-count variance law is a heuristic for scrambled quasi-Monte Carlo, not a
guarantee of optimal allocation or valid intervals.

Precision, coverage and reproducibility answer different questions. Precision
requires discrepancy plus numerical uncertainty to fit the tolerance. Coverage
checks whether numerical intervals contain known answers over repeated trials.
Reproduction checks whether saved inputs and code produce identical bytes. None
identifies individual causal effects or proves that QB/team value is separated.
