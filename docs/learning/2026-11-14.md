## What changed and why

The old solver adds a tiny prior precision to a large cross-product matrix.
Floating-point addition can lose the tiny quantity. When a QB effect, year effect
or team-wide shift has exactly the same observed consequence as another modeled
combination, the prior supplies essential information along that direction.
Losing it can make a mathematically positive system appear indefinite.

Plain QR avoids squaring the condition number but passes only 61/72 demanding
controls. The preferred solver first proves exact binary dependencies using
rational arithmetic. It integrates those proper-prior directions analytically,
then factors the remaining augmented rows by QR. Their uncertainty is restored,
including covariance with fixed context. Nothing is rounded into an alias;
near aliases stay in the model. No players are dropped or forced to have zero
value. Different precision blocks remain separate.

The 80- and 110-digit reference calculations directly build the original small
models, including game effects. All 72 reference pairs agree and all 72 preferred
candidate checks pass. Archived normal equations pass 28/72. This is an arithmetic
comparison, not a claim that the tested data are NFL truth. The near-alias residual
error is close to its strict threshold, so the regression gate must remain active.

Every one of the 85 declared archived comparisons passes. At the exact failed
point, raw-row and reordered-basis checks agree with the compressed solver. The
old exception reproduces, while the new fit completes in 4,938 evaluations and
41.2 seconds. Its negative training log-score gain is retained; completion does
not mean the proposal is uniformly better. No favorable restart is selected.
