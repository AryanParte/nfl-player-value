## Decisions and concepts

**Numerical precision is not statistical calibration.** The reference asks whether
we calculated the specified posterior reliably. Calibration asks how its intervals
behave against known generated truth. Observational attribution asks what the
QB/team allocation assumptions support. Software correctness and exact replay do
not answer the latter questions. No new calibration draws or Week 10 outcomes were
used, and no interval was widened.

An interval endpoint is checked through its **CDF**, the probability below it.
The nominal lower/upper targets are 0.025/0.975. An accurate mean does not establish
accurate endpoints: Gauss–Hermite's basic moment errors are below 6.46e-11, but its
interval control still fails. Nor is a quadrature error estimate a rigorous bound;
some nonconverged Legendre controls understate actual error. Those outcomes are
retained, and the frozen selection chooses neither tensor alternative.

The sparse rule combines nested open one-dimensional rules. Exact integer-angle
keys reuse evaluations, while streamed batches avoid storing every large vector
of player functionals. Signed sparse-grid weights are integration coefficients,
not player opportunities or posterior probabilities. Both mean and normalization
error remain in the CAVAA contrast check. No benchmark, workload, prior or scoring
population changed to obtain a more favorable result.

The data have two different clocks: the 2026 game cohort closes at
`2026-11-08T01:41:17.891273Z` (November 7, 20:41 Eastern), while the later evidence
cutoff is `2026-11-08T02:47:32.067979Z`. Neither is November 12 live coverage.
Retrospectively retrieved roster evidence is not proof it was available before
kickoff. Those provenance limits remain visible in the saved source reports.

### Decisions and statistical concepts

**Numerical uncertainty is different from player uncertainty.** For example, a
QB's estimated contribution has statistical uncertainty because football data
cannot perfectly separate him from his team. The new reference asks a narrower
question: did we calculate that specified statistical model accurately? Passing
the numerical check would not establish that its QB/team allocation is correct.

**Randomize whole integration nets.** Points within a Sobol net are dependent.
The study uses 32 independently randomized nets, retains their mass and weighted
mean/CDF integrals, and estimates covariance across those replicates. A posterior
mean is a ratio: numerator and normalization move together. Fieller inversion
accounts for that covariance and explicitly rejects unbounded ratios. It preserves
negative and zero contributions. The confidence construction is approximate;
known-answer coverage must be measured rather than asserted.

**A proposal is a computing device, not a football prior.** The initial local-t
and prior-mixture proposals fail precision. A separately frozen adaptation fits
a mixture to target-density-only pilot points and corrects every new evaluation
by the exact sampling density. It does not train on CAVAA outputs, known answers
or the primary calculation's samples. No player interval or model prior changes.

The adapted method passes three initial fixtures but fails tail coverage. Review
finds that the initial 128-trial lower-bound gate has only 54.07% pass probability
even at true 95% joint coverage. That does not excuse the failed result. A new
pre-result plan freezes 1,024 trials on each of the four exact same proposals,
fresh randomizations, every original gate and an additional joint-undercoverage
test. No optional stopping or follow-on retry is allowed. This larger study
passes, with measured joint coverage 94.34–95.31%; its binomial intervals remain
visible. Empirical evidence on four functions is not a universal guarantee.

**Separate disagreement from an inconclusive reference.** At the real QB's worst
paired check across benchmarks and prior settings, point discrepancy is 0.005394
posterior SD and numerical error is 0.024879 SD. Their total, 0.030273 SD, fails
the 0.01 limit. The failure cannot establish that the primary estimate is biased. The
team-only reference passes, and the hierarchy stress pilot has just 5.8 effective
samples out of 4,096 with every mixture-fitting start at its iteration cap.
These findings motivate a density-only holdout test of proposal instability;
they do not justify relaxing accuracy limits or declaring causality.
