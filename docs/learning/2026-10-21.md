## Statistical concepts

Comparing models requires a common sample. If the rich model sees easier games
than the reduced model, their difference confounds information loss with schedule
composition. Both views now use the exact same game/play keys and outcomes.

The rich view retains all 22 player IDs and preplay state. The reduced view retains
team-game outcomes, average context and defender appearance fractions. A defender
on 30 of 60 eligible pass/run plays receives a 0.5 workload fraction, not half the
team's EPA. The target is signed team EPA (`-offense EPA`), and no player receives
an allocated score at this stage. Keeping the outcome separate from attribution
avoids awarding the same play value to eleven teammates independently.

Whole-game exclusion is conservative but can bias the sample. One missing or
nonstandard lineup removes the game from both views, preserving the denominator
at the cost of coverage. We must examine which teams and game states are lost.
This controlled reduction is not yet a replica of actual live 2026 missingness.
Pressure and time to throw are deliberately absent as adjustments because they
can partly reflect player-created value. Even preplay state is observed context,
not proof of the total causal effect of replacing a player.

## Engineering decisions

Reused the standard-library content-addressed store; no new runtime dependency.
FTN participation has separate CC-BY-SA terms, so receipt version 2 archives both
the legal text and nflreadr's actual source declaration. The generic archive's
CC-BY license must not silently replace those terms. Earlier source receipts stay
version 1 and replay unchanged. Data attribution is carried into both derived views.

The adapter rejects duplicate keys and malformed identity structure. Nonstandard
lineups remain in raw archives with issue counts; they are not filled with guessed
players or zero contribution. The builder checks possession, both teams, 11 distinct
IDs per side, disjoint lineups and the complete player union. Canonical formatting
alone does not verify a roster identity, position or assignment.

## Observed 2025 findings

- FTN participation: 45,184 rows in 285 games; 45,005 pass lineup structure checks.
  The source release contains no 2026 participation asset.
- PBP: 48,771 rows. All participation keys join to PBP. The other direction has
  3,587 unmatched PBP rows, including administrative events; missingness is audited
  before deciding which rows are research opportunities.
- Schedule: 272 REG games. The strict completion rule accepts 270. Two have an
  end-game marker and matching scores but a higher numeric play ID for an earlier
  event: KC–JAX Week 5 (timeout) and NE–BAL Week 16 (kneel). A numeric key is not
  guaranteed chronological order. These two remain explicitly excluded under v1;
  a versioned correction is the next prerequisite before fitting.
- In reconciled games, 32,575 candidate plays contain 37 nonstandard lineups across
  29 games. These are count failures, not proof that the source is erroneous;
  illegal personnel can occur. Complete-game selection also removes 3,479 otherwise
  matched plays from those games.
- Retained: 29,059 plays, 241 games and 482 defensive team-game rows. All 32 teams
  appear, with 12–17 retained games each. There are 1,026 distinct GSIS IDs on the
  defensive side; this is not a count of qualified or position-validated defenders.
